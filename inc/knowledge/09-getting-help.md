# Getting Help

If something isn't working as expected or you're not sure how to do something, here's how to get the right help quickly.

---

## What to Include When Reporting an Issue

The more detail you provide, the faster it can be resolved. When reaching out, try to include:

- **What you were trying to do** — e.g. "I was trying to update the phone number in Site Settings"
- **What happened instead** — e.g. "The page went blank after I clicked save"
- **Where it happened** — the page name or URL
- **A screenshot** if possible — even a photo of your screen on a phone is helpful

---

## Content Changes vs. Technical Issues

**Content changes** are things you can handle yourself using these help articles — updating text, swapping images, adding a collection entry, and so on. That said, if you'd rather just send us a message and have us take care of it, we're always happy to help.

**Technical issues** are anything that looks broken, behaves unexpectedly, or requires a change to the design or structure of the site. These should always be sent to your developer.

Examples of technical issues to escalate:

- Something on the site looks visually wrong or broken
- A form isn't sending emails
- A page is showing a white screen or an error message
- You need a new section, page layout, or feature added

---

## Things to Avoid When Troubleshooting

It can be tempting to click around to try and fix something, but some actions in WordPress are hard to undo. If something seems wrong:

- Don't install or delete plugins
- Don't change **Settings → Permalinks**
- Don't delete pages, even if they look unused — check with your developer first
- Don't switch or deactivate the theme

When in doubt, leave it as-is and get in touch — it's much easier to fix a problem than to undo an accidental change.
