# SEO & Search Visibility

Before your site launched, we configured the SEO foundations so your pages are set up correctly for search engines from day one. This article explains what was done and what you can maintain or update yourself over time.

---

## What We Set Up at Launch

Good SEO starts with a solid technical foundation. Before launch we took care of the following:

- **Page titles and meta descriptions** — every key page has a unique title and description written to accurately represent the content and encourage clicks from search results
- **Open Graph images** — the images that appear when your pages are shared on social media
- **Site structure** — clean URLs, logical page hierarchy, and correct heading structure throughout
- **XML sitemap** — automatically generated and submitted to help search engines discover and index your pages
- **Canonical tags** — prevents duplicate content issues by telling search engines which version of a page is the primary one
- **Schema markup** — structured data that helps search engines understand your content and display richer results
- **No-index rules** — admin, login, and other non-public pages are excluded from search results
- **Redirects** — any old URLs are redirected correctly so no links are broken

## What You Can Update Yourself

As you add new content — blog posts, service entries, team members — each new page has its own SEO settings you can fill in.

### Editing a Page's SEO Title & Description

1. Open the page or collection entry in the editor.
2. Scroll down below the content to find the **SEO** panel (look for the SEOPress section).
3. Under the **Titles** tab, update the **SEO Title** and **Meta Description** fields.
4. You'll see a live preview of how the result will appear in Google search results as you type.
5. Save or update the page when done.

**Tips for writing good titles and descriptions:**

- Keep the SEO title under 60 characters so it doesn't get cut off in search results
- Keep the meta description under 160 characters
- Include the main topic or keyword naturally — don't stuff it
- The description should read like a summary that makes someone want to click

### Setting a Social Share Image

When someone shares a page on Facebook, LinkedIn, or similar platforms, the image shown can be controlled per page.

1. Open the page or entry in the editor.
2. Scroll to the **SEO** panel and click the **Social** tab.
3. Upload or select an image under **Facebook Image** — this also applies to most other platforms.
4. Save or update the page.

If no custom image is set, the page's featured image is used automatically. If there's no featured image, a site-wide default image is used.

## Ongoing SEO Considerations

Technical setup is only part of the picture. The most impactful things you can do over time are straightforward:

- **Keep content accurate and up to date** — outdated information signals to search engines that a page isn't being maintained
- **Write naturally for your audience** — pages that genuinely answer questions tend to rank better than pages written around keywords
- **Add new content regularly** — blog posts and updated service pages give search engines more to index and more reasons to revisit your site
- **Use descriptive image alt text** — see the **Accessibility & Images** article for guidance
