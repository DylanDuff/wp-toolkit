# Performance & Caching

If you've ever updated something on your site and not seen the change straight away, caching is usually why. This is normal and by design — understanding how it works will save you a lot of head-scratching.

---

## What is Caching?

To keep your site loading quickly for visitors, the server saves a stored version of each page and serves that instead of rebuilding the page from scratch on every visit. This stored version is called a **cache**.

The trade-off is that when you make a change, visitors might briefly see the previous version until the cache refreshes with the updated content.

---

## When Will My Changes Appear?

In most cases the cache clears automatically within a few minutes of saving a change. For most content updates — text, images, collection entries — you won't notice any delay at all.

If you save a change and don't see it reflected on the live site:

1. **Wait a few minutes** and refresh the page
2. **Hard refresh** your browser — press `Ctrl + Shift + R` (Windows) or `Cmd + Shift + R` (Mac) to bypass your browser's own cached version
3. **Try a different browser or incognito window** — sometimes your own browser is holding onto an old version

---

## If Changes Still Aren't Showing

If you've waited and the change still isn't appearing, the cache may need to be cleared manually. Get in touch and we'll take care of it — let us know which page is affected and what the change was.

> Don't try to fix a caching issue by making the same change multiple times — the content is saved correctly, it's just the display that needs refreshing.

---

## A Note on Page Speed

Your site is configured for performance — images are optimised, caching is in place, and the code is kept lean. If you ever notice the site feeling noticeably slower than usual, it's worth flagging to us rather than assuming it's normal. Sudden slowdowns can sometimes indicate an issue worth investigating.
